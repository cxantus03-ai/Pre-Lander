I'm about to say something that won't make me popular with the oral care industry.

My name is Sarah. I'm not a dentist. I'm not a periodontist. I'm the co-founder of Allpure — and until 14 months ago, I was just a daughter, watching her mother disappear behind her own hand.

My mom, Grace, is 61. She raised three kids on her own, ran a small catering business out of our kitchen for over a decade, and has never once in my life turned down a photo.

Then her gums started receding.

It crept in slowly — a little more tooth showing at the gumline every few months, a little pink in the sink when she brushed. Then it wasn't slow anymore. She was spitting blood every morning. Her front tooth had started to feel loose against her tongue. And she stopped smiling with her mouth open.

She missed my nephew's 6th birthday party at the park because she didn't want photos of herself with her lips pressed shut. She sat in the car "to answer an email."

I found her crying in the bathroom that night, pulling her lip down in the mirror, staring at gum tissue so red and swollen it looked like it was pulling away from her teeth.

"I'm 61 and I'm going to end up with dentures like my mother," she told me.

That's the night something in me changed.

What we tried first

Over the next year, my mom tried almost everything a person can try before surgery is even on the table:

  • Deep cleaning (scaling & root planing) — Scraped raw, sore for a week, pocket depths improved briefly, then crept right back within a few months.
  • Prescription antimicrobial rinse — Stained her teeth, dulled her sense of taste, and the bleeding returned within days of stopping it.
  • A "gum repair" toothpaste — Gritty, unpleasant, and after the promised "3-6 months," her pockets were no better.
  • Mouthwash rotation — Burned, dried her mouth out, and did nothing for the bleeding once she stopped using it daily.
  • Water flosser — Helped with food debris, did nothing measurable for the deeper pockets.
  • Diet changes, oil pulling, supplements — Still bleeding. Still receding.
  • Laser gum surgery, out of pocket — Weeks of soft-food recovery, moderate improvement that plateaued, still bled months later.

Nothing held. Every fix was temporary. And every temporary fix cost real money.

What we learned when we actually dug in

I'm not a scientist. But I spent months reading everything I could find, and talking to every formulator and herbalist who would take my calls. Here's the theory that finally made sense of why nothing was sticking:

The bacteria that drive gum disease don't just sit exposed in your mouth. They build a slimy, oily protective layer around themselves deep in the pockets between your teeth and gums — sometimes 6, 7, even 9 millimeters down. That layer is often described as hydrophobic, meaning water tends to bead up and roll right off it instead of soaking in.

Mouthwash is water-based. Water flossers are water-based. Even prescription rinses are water-based. If the shield protecting the bacteria repels water, a water-based product was never going to fully get through it.

Toothbrush bristles physically stop at 2-3mm. The infection often lives at 6-9mm. That's a gap no brush, paste, or rinse can close.

And even a deep cleaning that mechanically scrapes the surface doesn't change what's happening deeper in the pocket — which is why so many people are back in the chair a few months later.

What kept coming up, again and again, in traditional and herbal medicine literature, was a different approach entirely: oil-based botanical compounds, taken so they can reach tissue systemically rather than just sitting on the surface and rinsing away.

Two plants with a long history worth a second look

Two botanicals kept surfacing in that research, both with long histories of traditional use for oral and digestive wellness: wormwood and black walnut hull. Herbalists have used them for generations, often together, as part of traditional oral-care and cleansing routines — largely because, unlike water-based rinses, their active compounds are oil-soluble and traditionally believed to reach places a rinse can't.

We are not claiming a specific clinical mechanism as settled science, and Allpure Wormwood & Black Walnut is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease. What we can say is that this pairing has a long tradition of use, it's what we formulated around, and it's what our own customers keep telling us they've stuck with.

What changed for my mom

One thing I didn't expect: in month three, she ran out for about ten days while we waited on a new order. By day six, a little bleeding came back. By day ten, her gums looked inflamed again. The moment she resumed taking it nightly, things settled back down within about two weeks.

That's when it clicked for both of us: this isn't a "cure," and we'd never claim it is. Stop consistently supporting it, and old habits (and old bacteria) tend to creep back.

She hasn't missed a night since.
